What is a measure of location?04 / 40
What is a measure of location?
LO 3.1 · Measures of central tendency
Definition

A measure of location is a single value used to describe the central tendency of a set of data: the point around which the values gather.

The Coffee Address wants one number that answers: “How much does a branch sell on a typical day?” This chapter gives three main answers, and each one is useful in a different situation.

Remember from Chapter 1
A parameter describes a population. A statistic describes a sample.
01
The mean
The arithmetic average: add all the values and divide by how many there are. The most widely reported measure of location.
02
The median
The midpoint of the values after they have been ordered from minimum to maximum.
03
The mode
The value that occurs most frequently.

The population mean
LO 3.1 · Measures of central tendency
The formula
μ=N∑x​
01
μ (mu)
The population mean: a parameter.
02
N
The number of values in the population.
03
x
Any particular value.
04
Σ (sigma)
“Add the values”: Σx is the sum of all the values.

Use the population mean when you have every member of the group you care about: all the branches, all the employees, all the invoices of the month.

In words
Population mean = the sum of all the values, divided by how many values there are.
Excel=AVERAGE(range)the same function computes μ and x
A company has exactly 4 delivery vans that used 60, 55, 70 and 63 litres of fuel this week. Calculate the mean amount of fuel per van. Is the mean here a parameter or a statistic?
(60 + 55 + 70 + 63) / 4 = 248 / 4 = 62 litres per van. It is a parameter: the interest is these 4 vans, and every one of them is in the data.

LO 3.1 · Measures of central tendency
Definition

The median is the midpoint of the values after they have been ordered from the minimum to the maximum. For data with extreme values, the mean may not represent the centre fairly; the median does.

The values only need an order.

Only the middle position matters, not how extreme the ends are.
03
Splits the data in half
At least 50% of the values are ≤ the median and at least 50% are ≥ the median.

LO 3.1 · Measures of central tendency
Figure 2Where the mean, median and mode sit in symmetric and skewed distributions
Symmetric: mean = median = mode. Positively skewed (a long tail of large values, like profits or salaries): mode < median < mean. Negatively skewed (a long tail of small values, like easy exam scores): mean < median < mode. When the data is skewed, report the median; the mean chases the tail.

Growth over time: only the start and the end15 / 40
Growth over time
LO 3.1 · Measures of central tendency
The formula
GM=nvalue at the start of the periodvalue at the end of the period​​−1

n is the number of periods (here: years). You do not need the value of every year in between; the geometric mean fills the path with one steady rate.

Excel=(end/start)^(1/n) − 1
Why is the geometric mean better than the arithmetic mean for growth rates?
Growth compounds: each year multiplies the last, it does not add to it. The geometric mean multiplies the factors, so it matches how growth really works; the arithmetic mean overstates it.
Example: online store visitors

An online store had 10,000 visitors in January 2021 and 75,000 visitors in January 2026 (5 years later). What was the average growth per year?

GM=510,00075,000​​−1=57.5​−1
=1.496−1=0.496=49.6% per year

The store's traffic grew at an average rate of 49.6% per year: each year had about 1.5 times the visitors of the year before.

Q1
An accounting firm has 11 accountants who prepared 58, 75, 31, 58, 46, 65, 60, 71, 45, 58 and 80 tax returns last year. Find the mean, the median and the mode.
Mean = 647/11 = 58.8 returns. Sorted, the 6th value is the median: 58. The mode is also 58 (it occurs three times). The three measures are close, so the data is roughly symmetric and any of them is a fair report.
Q2
A hospital employs 50 nurse's aides at ê 12 an hour, 50 practical nurses at ê 20 an hour and 100 registered nurses at ê 29 an hour. What is the weighted mean hourly wage?
(50 × 12 + 50 × 20 + 100 × 29) / (50 + 50 + 100) = (600 + 1,000 + 2,900) / 200 = 4,500 / 200 = ê 22.50 per hour.
Q3
Compute the geometric mean of these five monthly percent increases: 8%, 12%, 14%, 26% and 5%.
Factors: 1.08, 1.12, 1.14, 1.26, 1.05. Product = 1.824; the 5th root is 1.128, so GM = 12.8% per month (the arithmetic mean, 13%, is slightly larger, as always).

Monthly salaries at a small office are 8,000, 8,500, 9,000, 9,500 and 60,000 (in riyals). Which measure of location best describes a typical salary, and why?
The median, ê 9,000. The outlier (60,000) pulls the mean up to 19,000, which describes nobody; the median is not influenced by the extreme value.

What is the variance?
LO 3.2 · Measures of dispersion
The idea

The variance is a measure of dispersion. It looks at the deviations of the data around the mean: how far each value sits from the balance point.

You can think of the variance as the mean of the squared distances from the mean: measure every distance, square it, and average the squares.

In words
Variance = the average squared distance from the mean.

Squaring does two jobs: it makes every distance positive (they no longer cancel to zero), and it gives extra weight to values far from the mean.

Figure 4The same four values as Figure 1: each distance from the mean is squared, and the variance is their average
QUA 107 · Chapter 3 · Describing Data: Numerical MeasuresVariance
The population variance21 / 40
The population variance
LO 3.2 · Measures of dispersion
The formula
σ2=N∑(x−μ)2​

σ2 (sigma squared) is the population variance: the average of the squared deviations from the mean. It uses every value, not just two. Its units are the units of the data, squared (riyals²), which is hard to read; the next slide fixes that.

Excel=VAR.P(range)the .P is for a Population
Why square the deviations instead of just adding them?
Without squaring they always add up to zero (the balance-point property), so the sum tells us nothing. Squaring makes every deviation positive and gives extra weight to values far from the mean.
01
Find the mean μ
Add all the values, divide by N.
02
Find each deviation
(x − μ) for every value.
03
Square each deviation
(x − μ)2: negatives become positive.
04
Sum the squares
Σ(x − μ)2.
05
Divide by N
The result is the variance σ2.

For a sample we use x instead of μ, and we divide by n − 1 instead of N.

01
Why n − 1?
Dividing by n tends to underestimate the population variance, because a sample rarely catches the extreme values. Dividing by n − 1 corrects this.
02
Which letters where?
Population: μ, N, σ2, σ. Sample: x, n, s2, s.
A quality inspector measures a sample of 25 bottles. What goes in the denominator of the variance?
n − 1 = 24, because it is a sample, not the whole population.

LO 3.2 · Measures of dispersion

The standard deviation becomes truly useful when it tells us how much of the data is around the mean. Chebyshev's theorem works for any distribution, whatever its shape:

Chebyshev's theorem
At least 1−k21​ of the observations fall within k standard deviations of the mean (for any k > 1).

“At least” means it is a guaranteed minimum: the true share is usually higher.

A market research firm in Riyadh studied the daily sales of an electronics store over the last year. The mean daily sales were ê 50,000 with a standard deviation of ê 5,000. At least what percentage of days had sales within 2.5 standard deviations of the mean?

1−2.521​=1−6.251​=1−0.16=0.84

A student scored 95. Is that unusually high?

Ethics: reporting results honestly30 / 40
Ethics: reporting results honestly
LO 3.2 · Measures of dispersion

Every measure in this chapter can be used to inform, or to mislead. A salary ad that says “average salary ê 19,000” is technically true even when one manager earns ê 60,000 and everyone else earns around ê 9,000.

The rule
Choose the measure that describes the data fairly, not the one that flatters it.
01
Report the right centre
Mean for symmetric data; median when the data is skewed or has outliers.
02
Report the spread too
A mean without a standard deviation hides half the story.
03
Be transparent
Say which measure you used, and acknowledge the limitations of your analysis.
04
Stay independent
Present results objectively, regardless of what answer the audience hopes for.

Quartiles, deciles and percentiles
LO 3.3 · Measures of position

The standard deviation describes the spread around the mean. Measures of position divide the ordered data into equal parts, so we can say where one value stands relative to all the others.

01
Quartiles Q1, Q2, Q3
Divide the data into 4 equal parts. Q1: 25% of the values are below it; Q2 = the median; Q3: 75% are below it.
02
Deciles D1 to D9
Divide the data into 10 equal parts. D5 = the median.
03
Percentiles P1 to P99
Divide the data into 100 equal parts. P25 = Q1, P50 = Q2 = the median, P75 = Q3.
Think of it as
Lining up the students of a class by height, then cutting the line into equal groups.

Examples: “our branch is in the top quartile for sales”, “this applicant's test score is in the 90th percentile”, “the median salary” (the 50th percentile).

Your score is at the 80th percentile. What does that mean?
About 80% of all scores are below yours (and about 20% are above). It does NOT mean you answered 80% correctly.

Locating a percentile: the location formula34 / 40
Locating a percentile
LO 3.3 · Measures of position
Step 1 · sort

Sort the data from the smallest to the largest value.

Step 2 · find the location
Lp​=(n+1)100p​

p is the desired percentile (for Q1 use p = 25, for the median 50, for Q3 use 75); n is the number of observations. Lp is a position in the sorted list, not a value.

Excel=QUARTILE.EXC · =PERCENTILE.EXCthe .EXC versions use the same (n + 1) method as this formula
Example: a whole-number location

Values: 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 (already sorted, n = 5). Find the median (p = 50).

L50​=(5+1)×10050​=3

The location is the whole number 3, so the median is simply the 3rd sorted value: P50 = D5 = Q2 = 16.

A class has 25 students. Which position holds the median score?
L50 = (25 + 1) × 50/100 = 13: the 13th sorted score.

When the location is not a whole number35 / 40
When the location is not a whole number
LO 3.3 · Measures of position

If Lp is not a whole number (say 3.5), the percentile sits between two values, and we interpolate:

01
The integer part
Points to the first of the two values (position 3).
02
The decimal part
Is the fraction of the gap to add (0.5 of the way to position 4).
The interpolation
value at the integer position + decimal part × (next value − value at the integer position)
Example

Values: 12, 14, 16, 18, 19, 20 (sorted, n = 6). Find the median (p = 50).

L50​=(6+1)×10050​=3.5

Position 3 holds 16 and position 4 holds 18. Go half way:

median=16+0.5×(18−16)=17
Same six values: find Q3 (p = 75).
L75 = 7 × 0.75 = 5.25. Position 5 holds 19, position 6 holds 20: Q3 = 19 + 0.25 × (20 − 19) = 19.25.

With n = 15 branches, (n + 1) = 15 + 1 = 16. All three locations (16 × 25/100 = 4, 16 × 50/100 = 8, 16 × 75/100 = 12) are whole numbers, so no interpolation is needed. Sorting first is the step students forget most often.

LO 3.3 · Measures of position
Q1
What is the difference between the 50th percentile, the 5th decile, the 2nd quartile and the median?
Nothing: P50 = D5 = Q2 = the median. They all cut the ordered data in half.
Q2
Salaries of a sample of 9 employees, sorted (in '000 riyals): 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 18, 22. Find Q1 and Q3.
L25 = 10 × 0.25 = 2.5: Q1 = 9 + 0.5 × (10 − 9) = 9.5. L75 = 7.5: Q3 = 15 + 0.5 × (18 − 15) = 16.5 (thousand riyals).
Q3
In the 15-branch example, what percent of branches serve MORE than 220 customers a day?
220 is Q3: 75% of the values are below it, so about 25% are above.
Q4
For the 15 branches, find the 90th percentile of daily customers.
L90 = 16 × 0.90 = 14.4. Position 14 holds 231, position 15 holds 240: P90 = 231 + 0.4 × (240 − 231) = 234.6 customers.
