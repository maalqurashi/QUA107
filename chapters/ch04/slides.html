From five numbers to a picture
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry
Remember from Chapter 3

Five values give a quick overview of a whole distribution, the five-number summary: the minimum, Q1, the median, Q3 and the maximum. For the 15 branches of Chapter 3 they were 146, 172, 204, 220 and 240 customers.

A box plot (also called a box-and-whisker plot) draws these five numbers as one picture. In one glance it shows the centre, the spread and the shape of the data, and it flags unusual values.

The idea
Five numbers in, one picture out: centre, spread, shape and outliers together.
01
Centre
The line inside the box is the median.
02
Spread
The box holds the middle 50% of the values; the whiskers show the usual range.
03
Shape
A median off centre and unequal whiskers mean skew.
04
Outliers
Values unusually far from the rest are drawn as separate points.

Example: a bank compares the waiting times of its branches with one box plot per branch, side by side on the same scale.

The interquartile range (IQR)06 / 32
The interquartile range
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry
The formula
IQR=Q3​−Q1​

The interquartile range is the distance between the third and the first quartile: the length of the box. It is the range of the middle 50% of the values.

Why managers like it
Outliers cannot touch the IQR: it ignores the extreme 25% on each side.
Excel=QUARTILE.EXC(range,3) − QUARTILE.EXC(range,1)the .EXC quartiles use the same (n + 1) method as Chapter 3
Example: daily customers at 15 coffee branches

The Coffee Address recorded the daily customers of its 15 branches. Sorted, with (n + 1) = 16, the quartiles sit at positions 4 and 12:

Table 1The 15 daily customer counts, sorted (Q1 and Q3 highlighted)
146147163172175178194204205205209220228231240
IQR=Q3​−Q1​=220−172=48
The full range of the same data is 240 − 146 = 94. Why is the IQR (48) so much smaller?
The range uses the two most extreme branches only. The IQR drops the lowest 25% and the highest 25%, so it describes the typical middle half without the extremes.

Outliers: the 1.5 × IQR rule07 / 32
Outliers: the 1.5 × IQR rule
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry
Definition

An outlier is a value that falls unusually far from the other values in the data set. On a box plot, outliers are drawn as individual points beyond the whiskers.

The boundaries
lower boundary=Q1​−1.5×IQR
upper boundary=Q3​+1.5×IQR

Any value below the lower boundary or above the upper boundary is considered an outlier. The whiskers stop at the most extreme values that are inside the boundaries.

Excel=Q1 − 1.5*IQR and =Q3 + 1.5*IQRbuild them from the two QUARTILE.EXC cells
01
Why 1.5? A rule of thumb
There is no theorem behind 1.5. It is a rule of thumb: a practical rule based on experience, not on proof. It marks values far enough to be surprising, and almost all software uses it.
02
An outlier is a signal
A special event (a holiday doubled sales), an error in recording, or a real extreme case. Investigate it; do not delete it automatically.
For a data set, Q1 = 20 and Q3 = 40. What are the outlier boundaries?
IQR = 40 − 20 = 20. Lower boundary: 20 − 1.5 × 20 = −10. Upper boundary: 40 + 1.5 × 20 = 70. Any value below −10 or above 70 is an outlier.

The shape of a distribution11 / 32
The shape of a distribution
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry
Figure 4Four common distribution shapes
Symmetric: mean = median; the left side mirrors the right. Skewed right: a long tail of large values pulls the mean above the median (incomes, overtime hours). Skewed left: a long tail of small values pulls the mean below the median (scores on an easy exam, age at retirement). Bimodal: two peaks usually mean two different groups mixed in one data set. When the data is skewed, report the median; the mean chases the tail.

How skewness shows in a box plot
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry

Two questions tell you the shape from a box plot: Where is the median inside the box? and Which whisker is longer? Median near Q1 plus a long right whisker: skewed right. Median near Q3 plus a long left whisker: skewed left.

The overtime box plot (Figure 3) has its median near Q1, a long upper whisker and an outlier at 60. What is the shape?
Skewed right (positive skewness). Most employees work few overtime hours; a few work many.
QUA 107 · Chapter 4 · Describing Data: Displaying and Exploring DataPearson sk
Measuring skewness: Pearson's coefficient13 / 32
Measuring skewness: Pearson's coefficient
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry
The formula
sk=s3(xˉ−Median)​

A picture shows the shape; the coefficient of skewness measures it with one number, always between −3 and +3. It compares the mean with the median: the mean chases the tail, the median does not, so their distance reveals the skew.

Excel=3*(AVERAGE(r) − MEDIAN(r))/STDEV.S(r)Pearson's coefficient built from three Chapter 3 functions
01
Near 0
The distribution is roughly symmetric: mean ≈ median.
02
Positive (up to +3)
Mean above the median: skewed right. The larger the value, the stronger the skew.
03
Negative (down to −3)
Mean below the median: skewed left.
A data set has mean 62, median 70 and s = 12. Compute sk and describe the shape.
sk=123(62−70)​=12−24​=−2, a considerable negative skewness: a long tail of small values pulls the mean below the median.

Measuring skewness: the software method14 / 32
Measuring skewness: the software method
LO 4.1 · Box plots and measures of symmetry
The moment coefficient
sk=(n−1)(n−2)n​∑(sx−xˉ​)3

Statistical software uses a more sensitive recipe: standardize every value (how many standard deviations from the mean), raise each standardized value to the third power, and sum. Cubing keeps the sign: values far above the mean push the sum up, values far below push it down.

Excel=SKEW(range)one function does all the steps; new in this chapter
01
Standardize
Compute (x−xˉ)/s for every value.
02
Cube
Raise each result to the third power; the sign survives.
03
Sum and scale
Add the cubes and multiply by n/((n−1)(n−2)).

Reading the result is the same as Pearson: positive means skewed right, negative means skewed left, near zero means symmetric.

The sorted commissions of 11 sales agents (in '000 riyals): 8, 12, 15, 18, 20, 22, 25, 28, 30, 34, 40. Find the five-number summary and the IQR.
With n = 11, (n + 1) = 12: Q1 at position 3 = 15, median at position 6 = 22, Q3 at position 9 = 30. Five numbers: 8, 15, 22, 30, 40. IQR = 30 − 15 = 15 (thousand riyals).

Skewed left (negative skewness). Report the median: the mean is pulled down by the tail of small values.

The correlation coefficient r21 / 32
The correlation coefficient r
LO 4.2 · The relationship between two variables
The formula
r=(n−1)sx​sy​∑(x−xˉ)(y−yˉ​)​
01
r
One number for the direction and the strength of a linear relationship: when x is above its mean, is y usually above its mean too?
02
sx · sy · n
The two sample standard deviations (Chapter 3) and the number of paired observations.
Excel=CORREL(x_range, y_range)the same result as =PEARSON; new in this chapter
Figure 9The logic behind the formula: deviations on matching sides of the means multiply to +, opposite sides to −
A point where both variables sit above (or both below) their means gives a positive product (x − x)(y − y); opposite sides give a negative product. The numerator adds all the products: r is positive when the points climb, negative when they fall, near zero when + and − cancel.

Properties of r
LO 4.2 · The relationship between two variables
Figure 10The scale of the correlation coefficient, from −1 to +1
01
Always between −1 and +1
−1 and +1 are perfect straight lines; 0 means no linear relationship.
02
Sign = direction
Positive: the variables rise together. Negative: one rises while the other falls.
01
Distance from 0 = strength
0.877 and −0.877 are equally strong; only the direction differs.
02
No units
Correlating °C with riyals is fine: r is a pure number, and swapping x and y does not change it.
A study finds r = 0.85 between hours of study and exam scores. What can you conclude?
A strong positive linear relationship: students who study more tend to score higher. It does not prove that studying causes the higher score, and it does not apply to any single student.

What r cannot see

Figure 11Three warnings: r describes lines, misses curves, and can be created by one outlier
Three rules for safe use. First, r measures linear relationships only: a strong curve can hide behind r ≈ 0. From correlation to decisions
LO 4.2 · The relationship between two variables

The most important part of business statistics: what does the manager do with r = 0.877? The strong link between temperature and sales turns the weather forecast into a planning tool.

01
Smarter inventory
A hot week is forecast: order more ice cream, cones and toppings, and reduce the risk of running out.
02
Smarter staffing
Schedule more staff on the days forecast to be hot, instead of the same number every day.
03
Performance alarms
A day with 38 °C but LOW sales breaks the pattern: check the freezer, the stock, or a competitor's promotion.
The principle
Correlation lets the business be proactive instead of reactive: plan with the forecast, not after the queue.
Next Friday is forecast at 39 °C. Using the pattern of the 10 days, roughly what sales should the kiosk prepare for?
Around ê 2,000 or more: the two days near 38 and 39 °C sold 2,300 and 2,000. Prepare stock and staff for a strong day; the regression methods in QUA 207 turn this pattern into a precise prediction line.

Correlation is not causation27 / 32
Correlation is not causation
LO 4.2 · The relationship between two variables

We found a strong correlation between temperature and ice cream sales. Does the heat cause the sales? Here, mostly yes, and common sense supports it. But r alone can never prove it: it only measures how two variables move together.

01
A third variable
Ice cream sales and electricity use for air conditioning rise together. Neither causes the other: the heat drives both.
02
Reverse direction
Correlation has no arrow. The data alone cannot say which variable moves the other.
03
Coincidence
With enough variables, some pairs move together by pure chance.
The rule
r says “they move together”. It never says “this one causes that one”.
Cities with more mosques also record more traffic accidents. Does one cause the other?
No. Bigger cities have more of both: population size is the third variable behind the correlation.

Contingency tables: two categorical variables28 / 32
Contingency tables
LO 4.2 · The relationship between two variables
Definition

A contingency table (or cross-tabulation) classifies each observation by two categorical variables at the same time: one variable defines the rows, the other defines the columns, and each cell counts the observations with that combination.

Scatter diagrams and r need numbers. When both variables are nominal or ordinal (department and training status, region and payment method), the contingency table is the tool.

Read it in words
Each cell answers: how many observations are in this row group AND this column group?
01
Market research
Preferred flavour by age group: which flavour should be advertised to which group?
02
Human resources
Training status by department: where is the program working?
03
Sales analysis
Product line by region: what sells where?
A university cross-tabulates students' majors with their preferred study time (morning, afternoon, evening). What could the table reveal?
Whether some majors prefer particular study times: for example, if most business students prefer evenings, evening sections deserve more capacity.

The counts say one thing

By raw counts, Sales looks like the star: 63 completed trainings, more than any other department. IT looks ordinary with 36.

The rates say the opposite

No. Its not-completed rate is 27/90 = 30%, while Marketing's is 20/50 = 40%. Marketing needs help first; IT (90%) is the model to copy. The biggest count is not the weakest rate.

Q1
A logistics company finds r = −0.92 between the age of its trucks and their fuel efficiency. Interpret the sign and the strength.

r=(10−1)×4×6180​=216180​=0.83: a strong positive relationship.

Inside Sales: 63/90 = 70%. Share of all completed: 63/161 = 39.1%. The first divides by the column total, the second by the row total: always say which one you are using.
